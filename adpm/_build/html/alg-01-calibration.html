6.1.2. Numerical Weather Prediction Ocean Calibration of SAR \(\sigma_0\)

The aim of the Numerical Weather Prediction Ocean Calibration (NOC) ([26]) is to provide an additional calibration step to the SAR averaged normalized radar cross section (\(\sigma_0\)) values used to retrieve the wind vector. These values are generally averaged on a grid of a few kilometres (approximately \(1-2 \times 1-2\ \mathrm{km}^2\)).

This calibration procedure is not applicable to sea-state parameters, since these are derived spectrally. Therefore, an absolute \(\sigma_0\) calibration is not necessary before retrieval. Instead, inter-calibration among different sea-state parameter sources can be useful for comparison and validation; this can be achieved with triple collocation. Similarly, no additional SAR-specific calibration procedure is recommended for line-of-sight (LoS) current retrieval beyond the harmonized Doppler calibration already part of the l2o processor.

Inputs

  • SAR \(\sigma_0\) values used to retrieve the wind-vector field.

  • Collocated wind vector from numerical weather prediction (NWP) models.

Procedure

  1. Set up approximately \(10^7\) NWP-wind/SAR-\(\sigma_0\) collocations, to ensure that wind direction is sampled uniformly and that the wind-speed range is adequately represented. Correct \(\sigma_0\) for Faraday rotation, which arises from ionospheric propagation effects at L-band and lower frequencies. Obtaining this many collocations requires 1–2 months of acquisitions, depending on revisit frequency and coverage.

  2. Bin collocations by incidence angle, wind direction, and wind speed, with bin sizes of \(1^\circ\), \(1^\circ\), and \(1\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), respectively.

  3. Compute the expected (simulated) \(\sigma_0\) values using the appropriate geophysical model function (GMF):

    (6.1)\[\sigma_{0}^{W,\mathrm{sim}} = \mathrm{GMF}(\vec{u}^{\mathrm{NWP}}, \theta, f_c, \mathrm{pol})\]

    Here, \(\vec{u}^{\mathrm{NWP}}\) is the NWP wind vector, and \(\theta\), \(f_c\), and \(\mathrm{pol}\) are the SAR incidence angle, carrier frequency of the transmitted signal, and polarization channel, respectively. The intended model is an L-band GMF (LMOD), analogous to the CMOD7 GMF used for C-band SAR satellites such as Sentinel-1 ([27]). LMOD specification needs to be determined.

  4. Obtain the final calibration-coefficient profile by weight-averaging the ratios of simulated and measured \(\sigma_0^W\) values over wind direction and then wind speed:

    (6.2)\[\alpha(\theta) = \left\langle \left\langle \left\langle \frac{\sigma_{0}^{W,\mathrm{sim},ijk}(\theta)} {\sigma_{0}^{W,m,ij}(\theta)} \right\rangle_k \right\rangle_j \right\rangle_i .\]

    Here, \(\alpha\) is the calibration correction factor; \(k\) is the index of occurrences in the \(\{i,j\}\) bin; and \(j\) and \(i\) are the wind-direction and wind-speed indices, respectively. The angle-bracket operator denotes the ensemble average.

Iterate the procedure until \(\alpha(\theta)\) is close to 1, according to the selected threshold:

(6.3)\[\Delta_{\alpha} = \left| \frac{\sigma_{0}^{W,\mathrm{sim}}}{\sigma_{0}^{W,c}} - 1 \right| \leq \Delta_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{th}} .\]

The corrected \(\sigma_0^W\) values are:

(6.4)\[\sigma_{0}^{W,c}(\theta) = \alpha(\theta)\sigma_{0}^{W,m}(\theta) .\]

In logarithmic space:

(6.5)\[\sigma_{0,\mathrm{dB}}^{W,c}(\theta) = \alpha_{\mathrm{dB}}(\theta) + \sigma_{0,\mathrm{dB}}^{W,m}(\theta) .\]

Metrics

(6.6)\[\Delta_{\alpha} = \left| \frac{\sigma_{0}^{W,\mathrm{sim}}}{\sigma_{0}^{W,c}} - 1 \right| .\]

Outputs

  • Calibration coefficients: \(\alpha(\theta)\) (equivalently \(\alpha_{\mathrm{dB}}(\theta)\)).

  • Calibration residuals: \(\Delta_{\alpha}\).
